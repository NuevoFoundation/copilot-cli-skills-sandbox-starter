CHALLENGE 03: Meet the people behind the mission
FROM: Izzy Beltran, Creative Director and Master of Fun, Nuevo Foundation
CONTEXT: Workshop request featuring a real NF teammate, not a quoted message.
PRIORITY: Optional. The SKILL remains the required deliverable.

GO-TO
Izzy wants visitors to meet the people behind the mission.
Create a friendly team page with the 16 supplied NF team members. Each card
needs a name, NF role, short supplied blurb, and an original cartoon-style SVG
avatar. The real NF team page already uses cartoons; this is a local practice
interpretation, not a copy of that artwork.

GROUNDING
Read challenge-data/content.json: about.teamNotes, sources.team, and team.
Use exactly those names, roles, and NF-only blurbs. They are a source snapshot,
not a newly verified current directory. Do not browse for photos, copy full
biographies, invent quotes, or infer appearance or personal traits from names.
Nuvi, NF's robot mascot, is bundled at assets/Nuvi-Robot.svg. This is the
placeholder illustration on the team-feature card; you may also use it in
the team-page header. Keep it local and do not present Nuvi as a person's portrait.

SCOPE
Create team.html, add its link to the homepage's "Workshop pages" footer nav,
and extend styles.css. Generate local SVG cartoon characters with code; no
image-generation service is needed. Use abstract, friendly, gender-neutral
characters rather than claimed likenesses. There is one prebundled fallback
per person in assets/avatars if generation stalls. Say when you used a fallback.

ACCEPTANCE
1. All 16 supplied people appear once, with correct names, roles, and blurbs.
2. Every card loads a distinct local SVG. No broken images or external requests.
3. A visible note says avatars are illustrative characters, not portraits.
4. Names and long roles remain readable without clipping at 390px and 1440px.
5. Decorative avatars use empty alt text when the adjacent text identifies the
   card; the page remains understandable without images or mouse interaction.

USE THE SKILL
GitHub Copilot CLI should automatically use issue-lifecycle in your assigned
repo. Start a separate issue before editing and continue through the phases
authorized by the request. Implement, inspect all cards, and review the intended diff and staged
paths before authorizing a commit/push. Finish with the actual 16-card check,
viewport checks, and published branch evidence. Do not claim a production
directory update. If remote operations fail, leave online completion pending.
